Scanning a connector

Open Scan me, place the connector on the plate and take a photo. A crop box appears around the connector; drag it or its corners if it isn't framed well, then tap Use photo. You'll see the closest matches with their part numbers. If the top answer is right, tap This is it and your photo is added to the training set, so the next scan gets a little better.

If the result says it isn't sure, retake the photo closer, with the pins facing the camera and no glare. If it still can't tell, the connector may not have been trained yet.

Crimped, uncrimped and housings

The Training set switch at the top of Scan me and Train the model chooses between three completely separate sets: Crimped terminals with wire attached, Uncrimped terminals, and connector Housings. Each has its own connectors, photos and classifier, and a scan only compares against the set that's selected, so pick the right one before scanning.

  • Crimped: trim the wire to a short stub of the same length every time and keep it on the jig.
  • Housings: photograph them on the same face each time (for example, mating face up). A housing must fit inside the jig's grey area for its size to be measured.

Teaching the site a new connector

The model learns from example photos. Each photo you add becomes a reference it compares against, so better photos mean better recognition. No coding or retraining needed.

  1. Set up the scanning spot

    Place the connector on the lab's 3D-printed plate, roughly in the middle, with nothing else on it. The whole plate should be in the photo; the site crops away the bench around it automatically. If size measurement is switched on, line the crop box up with the jig's inner edges: the site uses the jig's known size to measure the part, which helps tell apart parts that look alike but differ in size.

  2. Create the connector type

    Open Train the model and add the connector with a clear name, such as "SMA female, panel mount". Add the part number if you have it.

  3. Take varied photos

    Variety matters more than quantity. Ten different shots teach more than fifty near-identical ones.

    • Rotate the connector and photograph it from the front, side, top and at an angle.
    • Shoot some with the cable attached and some without.
    • Vary the distance a little, but keep the connector filling most of the frame.
    • For look-alikes (male vs female, 4-pin vs 5-pin), add close-ups of the pin face or threads.
    • Take photos on the phones people will actually use for scanning.

  4. Save and check the count

    Each connector shows a row of pins that fill up as you add photos. Aim to fill the row before relying on the results.

20–50photos for connectors that look clearly different from everything else
100+photos for look-alikes that differ by gender, pin count or thread

On the phone: "Take photo" opens the rear camera. Hold the phone steady, tap to focus on the connector, and avoid harsh reflections on shiny metal.

Classifier

Trains a small model on your photos that learns which details tell your connectors apart, instead of only comparing photos. It tests itself on photos it hasn't seen and is only used if it does at least as well as photo matching. Retrain after adding photos.
